Price signal and company news

News sentiment is an experimental input. Positive headlines do not guarantee gains, and negative headlines do not guarantee losses. The agent may classify an opinion headline as positive or negative; that does not establish that a deal happened.

Forward validation

Forward validation includes raw model forecasts, including those that failed the historical gate. It scores the first saved forecast for each stock, target session and history window against the actual completed close. It compares the combined model with a no-change baseline. Direction accuracy is measured on those saved results; headline-classification accuracy is not stock-prediction accuracy.

How the trained model works

Random Forest, an LSTM, a learned mathematical equation, their equal-weight ensemble, regularized regression, boosted trees, extra trees and a feed-forward neural network are trained on longer historical price data, with separate 10-, 20- and 30-day feature windows. The LSTM reads the last five completed daily returns in order alongside market and news features. The mathematical equation learns log return as an intercept plus weighted features, then converts it to percentage change using 100 × (exp(log return) − 1). The combined forecast averages Random Forest, LSTM and mathematical log returns with weights of one third each. Model selection uses earlier chronological folds and may choose an individual model if it performs better. Ten separate trading dates calibrate the range, and the final ten dates test performance. Stock samples from the same date stay together, and future target dates are excluded from earlier training blocks.

Where sufficient historical news coverage exists, a competing model learns from GDELT news-tone features. Only completed UTC-day news buckets available before the feature cutoff are used. Current financial-headline sentiment is additional context; its score is not substituted for historical tone or given a hand-set return adjustment.

The quality gate requires an improvement over a no-change baseline, directional accuracy above 52%, and acceptable held-out interval coverage. Passing these tests does not guarantee future accuracy. Historical tests use today's top-25 cohort and reconstructed archives; live saved forecasts provide a stronger continuing check. Unavailable data, short histories or failed validation produce “No reliable signal”. No model can guarantee stock-price movements.
